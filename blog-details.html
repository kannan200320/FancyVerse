How to Style & Secure an 18th-Century Venetian Masquerade Gown

Isabelle Sinclair
• September 4, 2026 • 5 Min Read • 24 Comments
Share This Guide:

There is an undeniable, spine-tingling magic in fastening the laces of a gilded Venetian brocade ballgown. But as any seasoned gala chairwoman will confess, wearing 18th-century court attire requires more than simply slipping into a dress — it is an art of structural balance and effortless poise.

1. The Foundation: Hoop Petticoats & Under-Lining

Never step directly into a ballgown without testing your petticoat expansion first. Our rental packages arrive with collapsible flexible-steel crinoline hoops. Before securing the waist drawstrings, shake the petticoat gently to allow the tiers to drop evenly. The lowest hoop should sit approximately 3 inches above your shoe soles to ensure you do not step on your hem when climbing grand marble staircases.

"A properly laced corset should feel like a firm, comforting embrace across the thoracic ribs — never sharp or constricting at the lower diaphragm. If you cannot laugh comfortably, the bottom eyelets are pulled too tight."
— Isabelle Sinclair, Head Curator

2. The Corset Lacing Technique

Our gowns feature dual-direction spiral lacing. Begin by loosening the back laces completely until you can fasten the front concealed hook-and-eye placket. Once the front closure is secure, have a companion gently pull the top laces outward and down in an 'X' crossing pattern.

Always tie the final knot into a double bow tucked discreetly underneath the matching velvet peplum fold.

3. Securing Your Venetian Mask All Night

The secret to keeping a heavy papier-mâché or metal filigree mask in place without ruining your styled wig lies in cross-pinning. Tie the satin ribbons firmly at the crown of your head (not the nape of your neck), then slide two crisscrossed bobby pins directly over the ribbon knot into your hair base.
